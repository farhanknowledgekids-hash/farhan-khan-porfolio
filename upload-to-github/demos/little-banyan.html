Your child's first visit, step by step

Most children are nervous because they don't know what will happen. So we tell them.

  1. Meet the chair

    Your child rides the chair up and down and holds the little mirror before anyone looks inside.

  2. Count the teeth

    A quick, gentle look while sitting on your lap if they prefer. No instruments they haven't seen first.

  3. Brushing lesson

    We show your child, and you, the best way to brush at their age.

  4. A plan for parents

    You leave with a written summary, any treatment advised and its cost, with no pressure to decide that day.

Questions parents ask us

When should my child first see a dentist?

Around their first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth appearing. Early visits are short and mostly about getting comfortable.

Can I stay with my child during treatment?

Yes. Parents are always welcome in the room, and younger children can sit on your lap for check-ups.

Will I know the cost before treatment starts?

Always. You get a written estimate for any treatment before it begins, and nothing is added without asking you first.

Do you see dental emergencies?

Yes, during clinic hours. On Sundays, call the clinic number and the on-call dentist will advise you.

Book a visit

Send a request and the front desk will call you back to confirm a time, usually the same day.
